# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added
- *cab IR* unit: your own cabinet impulse response (a WAV, cut to 2048 taps, about 43 ms) convolved directly, with level and mix; one IR shared by every cab IR unit. Browser and plugin (the plugin keeps the path in the session). `src/dsp/cabir.ts` + `plugin/Source/dsp/CabIR.h`, null-tested.
- *NAM capture* unit (plugin only): loads a Neural Amp Modeler `.nam` file through the NAM core (MIT; built as its own C++20 library, `plugin/Source/NamHost.cpp`), with input and output trims, resampling when the model's rate differs from the host's. One mono model for the whole rack (stereo is summed). The web version passes the signal through and leaves it out of the library. `plugin/Tools/nam-test.cpp` checks a model.
- The library's sections start collapsed; "+ new" starts an empty rack.
- *envelope filter* (filter): an auto-wah. A rectifying envelope follower sweeps a resonant state-variable filter (low, band or high-pass; up or down) from your picking; sensitivity is in volts, so a real guitar and the demo respond alike.
- *pitch shifter* (modulation): -12 to +12 semitones and fine cents, with mix, by two crossfading read taps. The shifted signal is delayed about 30 ms, as in any granular shifter; the dry signal in the mix is not.
- Two rigs for them: *Quack Shop* (envelope filter into a clean amp) and *Twelve Strings* (octave up, amp, hall).
- Rig sharing: *share* copies a link with the rig in the address hash (compressed, base64url), *export* saves it as a `.ftrig.json` file, *import* opens a file; a `.json` dropped on the page and a pasted link or JSON also import. Everything that comes in is checked against the module library and clamped; a name that clashes with a factory rig gets "(shared)". Browser only (the plugin's page has no address bar or downloads).

### Changed
- The drive pedals rebuilt from their schematics, with each circuit's bandwidth limits inside its gain stages, before the clipping (measured against the pedal dossier; nobody has listened yet). The level knob is now the output pot: +12 dB is the circuit's own output in volts, 0 dB is 12 dB under it (it used to be a normalised "ceiling").
  - *glow* (TS808): Yeh's clipping-stage equation solved exactly by backward Euler each sample (it was the static approximation), the two diodes a factor 2 apart in Is (second harmonic -40 dB at 220 Hz/100 mV; it had none), the tone stage as its circuit (was one pole), the two 16 Hz input high-passes. Small-signal response within 0.31 dB of Yeh's A(s) and the tone circuit.
  - *rasp* (RAT): the LM308 as a real op-amp (1 MHz gain-bandwidth, 0.3 V/us slew as a smooth saturating integrator, soft rails) inside its feedback network with the 100 pF, solved implicitly; the hard slew clamp is gone; the filter knob is the 1.5k + 100k pot into 3.3 nF (18.5 dB at 4 kHz from end to end).
  - *muff* (Big Muff): an input stage that loads the pickup, the sustain pot, two clipping stages with 470k across 470 pF (a 720 Hz pole inside each stage: the dossier's "100k, 3.4 kHz" doesn't fit J.L.'s own Z2 figures, 470k does) and the diodes behind 1 uF solved exactly (the cap charges over a note), 16 Hz couplings (was 90 Hz), the Ram's Head tone stack as its network (6.5 dB scoop near 1 kHz at noon), the output stage; no low-pass patched on after the clipping. Inharmonic content at full gain from -43..-67 dB to -86..-97 dB.
  - *fuzz* (Fuzz Face): both transistors Ebers-Moll on a four-node solve with their junction capacitances as real capacitors (410 pF base-emitter, 20 pF collector-base, after Holmes et al.) and 0.3 uA of leakage; it idles at Q1 0.25 mA and Q2 0.44 mA; the output is the collector through 10 nF into the volume pot, no fudge gain or 15 kHz low-pass. At fuzz 4 it goes from -6 dB THD at guitar volume 10 to -40 dB at 6, and stays at -3 dB behind a buffer.
  - *transparent* (Klon): the gain stage after J. Chowdhury's analysis and his BSD-3 KlonCentaur code (values and topology; the code is our own): the pre-amp with feed-forward 1, the amplifier stage (13 to 28 dB, which settles the dossier's 13-28 vs "40 dB" conflict in favour of 13-28), the 1N34A clipper as a network with antiderivative antialiasing, feed-forward 2 through the gain pot's second gang, summed as currents into 392k || 820 pF on the charge pump's rails; the treble control as its circuit (+22.8 dB at 4 kHz from end to end). Full-gain aliasing -81 to -113 dB.
  - *metal*: each op-amp stage has its feedback cap's pole before its clip, a mid push between them, diodes to ground across 10 nF solved by backward Euler; the triple clip of a full-band signal is gone.
  - *octave*: the germanium curve has the same slope on both sides of zero (it had a kink that made harmonics at every level).
  - Worst alias products at full gain and 0.5 V, 1.37 / 2.8 / 4.1 / 5.9 kHz, before -> after: glow -67/-50/-48/-34 -> -96/-68/-69/-61, rasp -58/-46/-42/-33 -> -75/-58/-54/-40, metal -51/-44/-38/-34 -> -79/-66/-58/-55, octave -80/-58/-48/-34 -> -96/-65/-51/-40, fuzz -50/-45/-34/-36 -> -54/-45/-45/-36.
- *wah*: J. O. Smith's Cry Baby fit as its analog prototype (the same at any host rate) with his gain law: the band's power is now level from heel to toe (the toe was 2 dB louder with the dry signal, ~7 dB without), the stage saturates smoothly near 2 V (13.3 dB less gain at 1 V in; Holters measured 13), and first in the rack the 68k input loads the pickup.
- *squeeze*: the detector holds its peak 25 ms before releasing: at the fastest release a 100 Hz note had 0.9% distortion, now none.
- *guitar tone*: with both knobs at 10 it is transparent on a real guitar's DI (it loaded the pickup a second time: -5 dB at 4 kHz, -7.7 dB at 5-6 kHz); the knobs apply only what they change. Rigs that roll the tone back keep their measured response (0.65 -> 0.51 and so on); Dotted Light's open guitar stays at 10.
- Chorus, flanger, rotary, tape echo and stereo delay read their moving delay lines with 4-point Hermite interpolation (linear interpolation dulled and grained them). The rotary's drum turns against the horn, and the horn's Doppler swing is +-0.45 ms (a 15 cm horn). The spring's dispersion follows the sample rate (it moved from 4.8 to 9.6 kHz at 96 kHz).
- Rigs with drives retuned for the new circuits by what reaches the amp: where the pot allows, the same signal into the amp as before; the tube-screamer boosts at full level with the amp's gain making up what a real TS can't give (it was +14-21 dB over the guitar, the circuit gives ~+10); fuzz rigs (21-26 dB RMS under the pack) now -13 to -16. Neck Glow and Quicksilver measure within 1.3 dB of before in every band from <300 Hz to >6 kHz.
- Input calibration: a new *interface reference* setting (next to the input selector; a host parameter appended after the slots, not part of a rig, default 1 V peak = −13 dBFS, the level most interfaces read at their minimum gain). The plugin's track input, the browser's interface input and a looped DI file are scaled by it into volts before the rack; the demo guitar (already in volts) is not. Until now a guitar arrived 12-15 dB lower than the amps were built for. The input meter reads volts (−60 to +6 dBV, green band 0.1-1 V) and its clip light shows the interface itself clipping. Older sessions and saved rigs load with the default.
- "Both inputs" (plugin and browser) no longer averages left and right, which lost 6 dB with the guitar on one side: each side is weighted by its share of the power.
- The amps rebuilt stage by stage from the schematics in the research dossier: real coupling caps (0.022 uF into ~1M, 7 Hz) instead of stacked 90-200 Hz high-passes; bypass caps as cathode states, so partly bypassed stages shelve the bass and remember the bias; treble-bleed dividers (470k with 470 pF in the JCM800, 2000 pF in the Soldano) and bright caps across the gain/volume pots (strong at low settings); Miller poles from each grid's real source resistance (moving with the pot); the JCM800 cold clipper unbypassed (10k, 18 dB) and the Soldano's 39k stage with its 1000 pF plate cap; DC-coupled cathode followers with grid-current clipping before the stack. Into the modern (Soldano) voicing's last stage: −12 dB at 82 Hz and flat below (was −44.5 dB and falling); the bias shift after a hard burst recovers in 20-33 ms (was 0.8-2.7 ms).
- Tone stacks solved as their circuits with the driving stage's source resistance (a plate-driven Fender stack sees ~38k); documented values for the blackface, Twin, Bassman, JCM800, plexi, Soldano, Mesa and the Vox top boost (Yeh). The hi-watt uses a James-type network fitted to ampbooks' CP103 transition frequencies (its exact topology was not found); the orange keeps undocumented values.
- The power stage works into the selected cabinet's speaker impedance (voice coil plus the resonance in its box; the V30 is Macak's measured one) through the tubes' plate resistance and the feedback: +9.3 dB at the resonance and +6 dB at 4 kHz on a no-feedback amp, less with feedback. Presence takes feedback away (at most 1 + loop gain). The fixed 110 Hz "resonance" shelves and the per-voicing EQs in front of the first stage are gone.
- Cabinets are minimum-phase FIRs (~6 ms, built at start from each speaker's synthesised response: box resonance, low-mid dip, presence rise, break-up peaks and notches, a short reflection, mic proximity and edge darkening; five mic positions interpolated), with the box resonance shared with the power amp. No third-party IRs.
- The oversampler is three polyphase IIR halfband stages each way: flat to 20 kHz (was −3 dB at 20 kHz per pass) and ≥106 dB rejection of what would fold into the audio band (was 36-69 dB), 0.09 ms round trip.
- Rigs retuned for the new amps: voicing trims bring rigs to similar levels, and the crunch and lead rigs get the gain (and up to 6 dB of input) to reach their saturation; Neck Glow stays dark and mid-forward (hi-watt, more gain, treble 1, high cut 3.6 kHz), Quicksilver keeps its overdrive into the brit 800.
- Every pedal reworked from its circuit. *fuzz* is a two-transistor Fuzz Face solved as a circuit: lopsided, and it cleans up when the guitar-tone unit's volume (right in front of it) rolls back, as the real one does; first in the rack it sees the pickup, after another unit a buffer. *boost* is a germanium treble booster with its real input cap (`focus` from full range to the 5 nF treble setting) and the upper-mid push a guitar's coil gives it. *octave* rectifies before it clips, so the octave survives being pushed. *transparent* clips with 1N34A germanium diodes after a 130 Hz-1 kHz gain stage, with an active treble shelf. *metal*'s first stages clip at op-amp rails, the second with diodes in its feedback.
- Amps: presence and resonance work inside the power stage's feedback loop (turned up on a cranked amp they add bite, not only treble); the output transformer saturates on low notes at full power.
- *squeeze*: a 10 dB soft knee, a smoother detector (low-note distortion from 0.8% to 0.1%), and a release that slows after long, heavy compression. *wah*: the Cry Baby sweep (450 Hz to 2.2 kHz, Q from 8 down to 2). *guitar tone*: a Strat's pickup, cable and pots as a circuit: the tone first damps the pickup's peak, and only near 0 pulls it down to a ~650 Hz honk; the volume keeps its highs through a treble bleed.
- *chorus* and *flanger* as bucket brigades (triangle LFO, filters around the line, soft saturation). *vibe*: the Uni-Vibe's uneven stage capacitors under a lamp and photocells that lag. *tape echo*: record pre-emphasis (loud repeats darken first) and a random drift in the wow. Hall, plate and room: damping set per line (the tail no longer comes out 15% short of the decay knob), early reflections on the room; the spring's decay matches its knob. Cabinets fall away faster above 5 kHz (less fizz).
- Amps and drives modelled from the circuits: each preamp stage is a 12AX7 on its load line (Dempwolf-Zolzer triode, grid current charging the coupling cap), the power stage is push-pull or single-ended output tubes with phase inverter and negative feedback, and the glow, rasp, muff and metal clippers solve the diode equation exactly: low gain is clean (mostly second harmonic) and the master adds power-amp warmth instead of fuzz.
- Copper Kite, Slow Ember, Chalk Line, Brass Rail, Iron Orchard, Slate Roof, Dust Bowl, Purple Lens and Spin Cycle: less gain, less treble and presence (the factory leads sit near 0 dB at 2-4 kHz against 0.5-2 kHz, these 2-5 dB under it). The first three lead with a rolled-back guitar tone and 2 dB of input.
- Classic amp, lead channel: the first stage's gain floor is 2 (was 6), so the gain knob changes how it breaks up and not only how loud it is. The first stages of the newer boutique, brit 800, rectified and modern voicings were set the same way.

### Fixed (the amp and pedal audits; measured, nobody has listened)
- *fuzz*: behind any buffered unit the Fuzz Face's Newton solve diverged to NaN (from a 0.3-0.7 V chord at fuzz 0-0.2) and the whole rack stayed silent. The junction voltages are now limited the SPICE way (pnjlim), the solve takes up to 40 steps when it hasn't converged, and a failed solve keeps the last sample. Every slot also resets itself if its module ever produces a non-finite sample (it no longer reaches the slots after it), and its meter pegs instead of reading 0. Checked: the auditor's rack (gate, fuzz, plexi, hall), 120 fuzz runs that failed 22 times before, and a randomised stress of every module.
- Aliasing at 44.1/48 kHz: the oversampling now follows a target internal rate: 16x for glow, muff, boost and transparent, 32x for fuzz, rasp, metal and octave, 16x for the amps (8x at 88.2/96 kHz and up), with antiderivative antialiasing on every tube table (preamp stages, cathode follower, power stage) and the muff's output stage moved into the oversampled domain. Inharmonic power at full gain, 0.5 V, 44.1 kHz, treble up, 1.37/2.8/4.1/5.9 kHz: fuzz -47/-40/-39/-38 -> -79/-73/-71/-71 dB, rasp -64/-54/-49/-43 -> -94/-81/-75/-70, metal -64/-55/-52/-48 -> -100/-95/-91/-89, octave -86/-60/-47/-38 -> -109/-108/-100/-84, muff -56/-53/-60/-54 -> -101/-109/-106/-107. Amps at full gain and master, 0.3 V, 48 kHz: hot plexi -48..-42 -> -85..-81 dB, rectified -51..-45 -> -99..-93, orange -61..-41 -> -108..-89.
- The amps' power stage was never driven (no voicing reached full power at the default master, even at 10 V). It is now driven in volts: the master pot (an audio taper; amps that have none default it to full), the phase inverter's gain onto the output grids against the tubes' bias (6L6 -52/-50 V, EL34 -38 V, EL84 -10 V, 6V6 -20..-35 V); the output follows the preamp whatever the master. A blackface at gain 5 reaches full power from ~25 mV peak at 1 kHz (Robinette's Deluxe: ~63 mV). New *output* knob on every amp unit (after the cabinet) so a rig sets its loudness without moving the drive.
- Amp voicings: the clean Twin uses the 250 pF blackface treble cap (120 pF is the bright cap's value) and its bright cap is out (the switch's off position); the blackface's second stage is bypassed (Robinette); the tweed's first stage is a 12AY7; the top boost's treble is a log pot; presence is the AC30's Cut on the chime voicing (250k log, 4.7 nF across the inverter) and does nothing on the amps that have none (Twin, Deluxe, 5E3, Champ). The cabinets' presence bells and low-mid dips are halved/0.6 (they stacked +10-12 dB at 3 kHz over 400 Hz on top of the speaker load's own rise; now +5..+6 on the 4x12s).
- Pedals: the Big Muff's stages use a frequency-dependent loop gain (the 10k load of the next stage): against the auditor's transistor-level reference at sustain 5, 50 Hz +4.9 -> -1.3 dB, 3 kHz -6.9 -> -2.7, 10 kHz -9.5 -> -4.9; stage 1 swings lopsided (second harmonic -40 dB at 0.4 V, was below -80) and the output is upright. Fuzz, muff and booster no longer get the DI's coil-and-cable resonance twice (one shared guitar, `pickup.ts`, also used by the wah and the guitar-tone unit). The booster has its 10 nF output cap (no DC moving with the envelope). The TS drive pot is a real audio taper (101k at noon, was 176k) and its tone stage is prewarped; the RAT leaves its rails at its normal slew rate; the wah has no dry leak (a Cry Baby has none). The drive *level* knob reads dB under the circuit's own output (0 dB = full up; it read 0 dB at 12 dB under).
- Knobs glide (20 ms): a rig change or automation no longer steps the coefficients (the guitar-tone step that gave a 4.5 V burst now peaks at 0.22 V; the Klon treble's 1.5 V at 0.51 V).
- Reverbs: the spring reaches its decay knob (5 s measured 3.0 s, now 4.8-4.9 s), has 100 dispersion stages (was 36, Valimaki et al. use about 100) and two different springs; hall and plate T30 at 1 kHz within ~4% of the knob (were 10-15% short).
- Every rig retuned for the new gain structure: with a 0.15 V single-coil strum the clean and ambient rigs keep the power stage under full power, crunch rigs drive it to ~1.5x, lead and heavy to ~2.5x, and every rig's demo strum sits at -18 dBFS RMS. Neck Glow's low cut is 80 Hz (was 220) with more bass and the power stage working; the Muff rigs' level is 10 dB higher with their tone a little darker; the Klon rigs' treble at 6.
